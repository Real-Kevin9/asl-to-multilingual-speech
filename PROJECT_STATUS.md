### 1.1 The honest caveat on Objective 1

The proposal promised **≥50 signs** at **≥85%**. Two recognition tracks now exist:

1. **Alphabet (live webcam):** 28 classes, accuracy met (93.7% held-out own-camera).
2. **WLASL word-level:** 50 glosses via MobileNetV2+BiLSTM; vocabulary target met,

   is far above chance (~2%) and far below 85% — a real limitation on CPU with
   few samples per gloss, not a missing module.

Both belong in the dissertation as separate results. See section 4.2 / 6.

## 2. What has been built

### 2.1 Recognition — MobileNetV2 + BiLSTM hybrid

Four alphabet backends plus a word-level WLASL backend, selectable via
`modules/recognition/registry.py`:

| Backend | Corpus test (324) | Own camera, held out (205) |
|---------|------------------|----------------------------|
| `hybrid_user` *(default webcam)* | **99.1%** | **93.7%** |
| `hybrid` | 97.8% | 62.4% |
| `cnn_lstm` | 71.6% | 22.4% |
| `landmark_mlp` | 66.7% | 55.1% |
| `wlasl_video` | — | **16.2%** on 50-gloss WLASL val (117 clips) |

The hybrid architecture is a MobileNetV2 crop stream plus a two-layer BiLSTM over
landmark geometry (`modules/recognition/hybrid_trainer.py`), trained on 14,769
hand crops (11,814 train / 2,955 val), reaching 94.2% val accuracy and 0.937
macro-F1 on the corpus.

`hybrid_user` is that model fine-tuned on 309 of the user's own webcam samples,
with 205 held out. The gap between `hybrid` and `hybrid_user` on own-camera data
(62.4% → 93.7%) is the domain gap between corpus images and a real webcam, and it
is the single most important experimental result in the project.

**Method note:** the held-out split is reconstructed deterministically by
`modules/recognition/user_split.py`, shared by the fine-tune and the evaluation so
they cannot disagree. An earlier version of the evaluation scored `hybrid_user` on
its own training data; that leak was found and fixed before any number was
published (Update 9).

### 2.2 NLP — fine-tuned T5 on ASLG-PC12

`t5-small` fine-tuned on cleaned ASLG-PC12 gloss→English pairs
(`modules/nlp/trainer.py`), with the rule-based corrector retained as an offline
fallback. On 300 held-out pairs: **BLEU 57.6** versus **19.2** for the rules, a
+38.4 absolute and +200% relative gain against a +20% target.

### 2.3 Emotion — 4-class CNN on FER2013

A VGG-style ConvNet over 48×48 grayscale faces with in-model augmentation
(`build_emotion_cnn_v2`), trained on 18,350 samples, early-stopped at epoch 30 with
the best weights from epoch 22. Test accuracy **65.5%** on 3,937 images. Detected
emotion drives TTS prosody (rate and pitch).

The original three-block network (`build_emotion_cnn`, 63.7%) is retained as `v1`
and its artefacts kept in `models/emotion_v1/`, so the comparison is reproducible.

| Emotion | v1 recall | v2 recall |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| happy | 0.85 | 0.78 |
| angry | 0.56 | **0.71** |
| neutral | 0.55 | **0.62** |
| sad | 0.52 | 0.46 |

v2 is more even-handed: it gives up some accuracy on happy, the easy majority
class, and recovers substantially more angry and neutral. The two models agree on
only 66% of test images despite similar overall accuracy, which is why the
difference was tested rather than assumed (McNemar exact p = 0.023).

Sad remains the weakest class in both. Objective 4 is not met — see section 4.1.

### 2.4 Multilingual TTS with caching

gTTS for English and Nepali, with `deep-translator` for the English→Nepali step
(`modules/tts/synthesizer.py`). English and Nepali are synthesised in parallel, and
both the audio and the translation are cached to disk, so a repeated utterance
needs no network round-trip.

| Bucket | Measured |
|--------|----------|
| Startup (first utterance, loads TensorFlow + T5 + CNN) | ~3,625 ms, one-off |
| Gloss tail (NLP → emotion → TTS), p95 | **815 ms** (target 1,500 ms) |
| Same utterance from cache | ~1.4 ms |

Separating these three buckets matters: quoting a single blended figure would
either hide the cold start or misrepresent steady-state performance.

### 2.5 Orchestration and model selection

`pipeline/model_selection.py` inspects the artefacts on disk and picks the best
available model for each stage, so `scripts/demo.py` is a single entry point that
needs no flags to run the best configuration. It prints a banner reporting which
model was chosen for each stage and the accuracy behind that choice.

## 4. Known limitations

### 4.1 Emotion accuracy (Objective 4, unmet)

**65.5% against an 80% target.** An improvement pass (Update 11) took the model
from 63.7% to 65.5% — statistically significant by McNemar's exact test
(p = 0.023) but nowhere near closing the gap. The failure is concentrated in the
sad/angry/neutral triangle, not spread evenly: happy reaches 0.83 F1 while sad
sits at 0.53.

**A caution about how this is justified.** It is tempting to write that human
accuracy on FER2013 is about 65.5% and claim the model matches human performance.
That comparison does not hold. The 65.5% human estimate is for the **7-class**
task; this project uses an easier **4-class** subset, having dropped disgust, fear
and surprise, and chance is 25% rather than 14.3%. The two numbers are not
comparable and the report must not equate them.

The honest position is that 65.5% is a genuine shortfall, not a redefinition of
success. For context on the 7-class benchmark, the best single network without
extra training data reaches about 73.7%, and higher figures generally come from
ensembles, attention architectures or ImageNet pretraining. On an easier 4-class
split a well-resourced implementation would likely reach the low-to-mid 70s, so
80% is ambitious but not obviously impossible.

The binding constraints here are practical: CPU-only training, 48×48 grayscale
inputs, and no pretrained backbone. Those are the things to name in the
limitations section, alongside FER2013's well-documented label noise.

### 4.2 Vocabulary: alphabet + WLASL word-level

The live webcam path recognises **28 static alphabet classes**. Separately, the
proposal's **≥50 word-level signs** path is implemented with WLASL:

| Item | Status |
|------|--------|
| Dataset | Voxel51/WLASL top-50 glosses (C-UDA academic licence) |
| Architecture | MobileNetV2 (TimeDistributed) + BiLSTM (`build_video_model`) |
| Artefact | `models/recognition/wlasl_video_model.keras` |
| Entry points | `download_wlasl.py`, `train_wlasl.py`, `evaluate_wlasl.py`, `demo.py --wlasl-video` |

Held-out accuracy on this sparse subset (≈10–16 clips/class) is reported in
`logs/evaluation/wlasl_accuracy.json`. The ≥85% accuracy target remains hard on
CPU with so few samples per gloss; the ≥50-sign vocabulary target is met.

Dynamic letters `J`/`Z` are still excluded from the alphabet path (they need motion).

### 4.3 Single-signer adaptation

`hybrid_user` is fine-tuned on one person's hands, camera and lighting. Its 93.7%
almost certainly does not transfer to a different signer, and there is currently no
measurement of how much it degrades. The usability sessions will provide the first
evidence, since participants other than the developer will be signing at it.

### 4.4 Network dependency

gTTS and the translation step both require internet. The cache removes the cost for
repeated utterances but not for new ones, and a fully offline run degrades to
writing `.txt` files instead of audio.

### 4.5 Nepali quality is unverified

English → Nepali is machine translation followed by a machine voice. No native
speaker has assessed either the translation or the pronunciation. Half of
Objective 3 is therefore functionally demonstrated but not quality-assured.

## 6. Framing the gaps for the report

Two objectives will not be fully met, and both are better handled by confronting
them than by hedging.

**Emotion (65.5% vs 80%).** Do not claim parity with human performance; the
available human figure is for a harder 7-class task (see 4.1). The defensible
position is narrower and stronger: the shortfall is real, its cause is identified
(the sad/angry/neutral boundary, plus FER2013 label noise), the improvement attempt
was measured rather than asserted, and the remaining headroom needs pretrained
features at a resolution that CPU-only training cannot deliver. Support it with the
per-class breakdown and the v1/v2 comparison.

**Vocabulary (≥50 signs).** Met via the WLASL top-50 word-level track
(`wlasl_video`). Alphabet accuracy remains the strong live result; WLASL
accuracy (16.2%) is the honest shortfall to discuss alongside data sparsity.

Neither should appear as a surprise in the conclusion. Both belong in the
methodology as decisions with stated reasons.
